Email nurture sequence template

Quick answer

What is a good email nurture sequence?

A good nurture sequence delivers what the lead asked for immediately, then sends five to seven short emails over about two weeks: a common mistake, a case story, how working together looks, answers to objections, a direct offer, and a final yes-or-no email. It stops the moment the lead books or buys.

01

How to use this template

  1. Trigger it from opt-in, with email 1 sending immediately.
  2. Add an exit condition: booked or purchased removes the contact from the sequence.
  3. Write from a real person in plain text. Designed newsletters read like marketing and land in promotions tabs.
  4. Fill email 3 with a real case you can document, with numbers.
  5. Run it alongside texts. Email nurtures; SMS and calls book. Use the SMS follow-up templates for the first hours.

If your opt-in is a guide or checklist, read the lead magnet entry for how to tie emails back to it.

02

Customizing it by industry

  • Coaching and consulting: stretch to 10 emails, add a second case story and a short video. See funnels for consultants.
  • Local services: shorten to four emails over a week; buyers decide fast. Add seasonal reasons to act.
  • Mortgage and insurance: lead with education on the process and timeline, never rate promises.
  • SaaS: replace the case email with a use-case walkthrough, and make email 6 a demo or trial offer.
  • eCommerce: turn it into a welcome flow: offer, bestsellers, reviews, founder story and a deadline.
03

Mistakes that make nurture emails fail

  • No exit condition. Sending "book a call" emails to someone already booked looks careless.
  • Several links and asks per email. One email, one action.
  • All value, no ask. Every email should make the next step easy, even the educational ones.
  • Generic case stories. Specific numbers and time frames persuade; adjectives do not.
  • Ignoring replies. A reply is a hot lead. Route it to a person the same day.

Frequently asked questions.

How many emails should a nurture sequence have?

Five to seven over about two weeks is a solid default for most lead funnels. Longer sales cycles, like coaching or B2B, can run 10 or more over a month. After the sequence ends, move non-buyers to a regular newsletter so you stay in touch without hard selling.

How often should nurture emails go out?

Daily for the first two days while interest is high, then every two or three days. The template sends on days 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 and 14. If unsubscribes spike after a particular email, check that email's content before changing the schedule.

Should nurture emails be plain text or designed?

Plain text for service businesses and high-ticket offers. It reads like a personal note, tends to reach the primary inbox, and invites replies. Designed emails suit eCommerce, where product images and buttons help. Either way, keep one clear link and a recognizable sender name.

What should I do when a lead replies?

Treat it as a hot lead. Remove them from the automated sequence, answer personally within hours, and offer a call time. Replies like 1, 2 or 3 to the final email can be routed automatically: book, set a 90-day reminder, or remove from sales emails.

Do I still need SMS if I have email nurture?

Yes, for booking. Email is good for education and proof over several days, but texts and calls are what get a lead on the calendar in the first hour. Use both: instant text and call at opt-in, then email nurture for leads who do not book.
