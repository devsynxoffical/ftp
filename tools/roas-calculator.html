Quick answer

How do you calculate ROAS?

ROAS is revenue attributed to ads divided by ad spend. $42,000 of revenue on $10,000 of spend is a 4.2x ROAS. Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by gross margin, so a 40% margin needs 2.5x to cover ad spend. Above that line, every extra dollar of spend returns profit.

01

How to use the calculator

  1. Ad spend: the total for the period across the campaigns you are judging.
  2. Revenue attributed to the ads: from your store, CRM or ad platform. If you rely on platform reporting, make sure Conversions API tracking is in place, since browser-only pixels miss sales.
  3. Gross margin: revenue minus product, fulfillment, shipping and payment costs, as a percentage.
02

Worked example with the default numbers

The calculator opens with $10,000 of ad spend, $42,000 of attributed revenue and a 40% gross margin:

  • ROAS: $42,000 ÷ $10,000 = 4.2x, or 420%.
  • Gross profit from ad revenue: $42,000 × 40% = $16,800.
  • Profit after ad spend: $16,800 − $10,000 = $6,800.
  • Break-even ROAS: 1 ÷ 40% = 2.5x.

Change the margin to 25% and the same 4.2x ROAS leaves only $500 of profit, because break-even rises to 4x. Margin decides how much ROAS you need.

03

Break-even ROAS formula and table

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin. At that ROAS, the gross profit from ad revenue exactly pays for the ads. To build in a profit target, use target ROAS = 1 ÷ (gross margin − target profit margin), with both as decimals.

04

How to read the results

Compare your ROAS with break-even ROAS, not with someone else's ROAS. If you are above break-even, you can usually scale spend and accept some ROAS decline. If you are below it, check attribution first, then the offer: an order bump or upsell raises revenue per buyer without raising ad cost, which is how low-ticket funnels like our $847K front end recovered 112% of ad spend on day zero.

05

What is a good ROAS?

A good ROAS is any ROAS above your break-even ROAS, with enough room left for the profit you want. There is no single number that is good for every business, because the same 3x is profitable at a 50% margin (break-even 2x) and loses money at a 25% margin (break-even 4x).

  • Find your floor: 1 ÷ gross margin. Below it, every sale from ads loses money.
  • Set your target: 1 ÷ (gross margin − target profit margin), from the table above.
  • Judge the trend, not one day: compare a week or more of spend, because attribution lags and a single day can swing either way.

A commonly quoted target is 4x. That only breaks even at a 25% margin, yet it is comfortably profitable at 50%, which is why your margin matters more than any rule of thumb. Use the calculator with your own margin, then compare with the category medians below.

Frequently asked questions.

How do I calculate break-even ROAS?

Divide 1 by your gross margin as a decimal. A 40% margin gives a break-even ROAS of 2.5x, a 25% margin needs 4x and a 60% margin needs about 1.67x. Below break-even, every sale from ads loses money after product costs, so fix margin, average order value or conversion before adding budget.

What is a good ROAS?

A good ROAS is one above your break-even ROAS with room to scale. For a 40% margin business that means comfortably above 2.5x. For a 70% margin service, anything above about 1.4x covers spend. Our Shopify clients commonly run 4.2x to 5.1x, but their margins set what that means in profit.

What is the difference between ROAS and ROI?

ROAS divides revenue by ad spend only. ROI divides profit by total investment, including product cost, staff, software and the funnel build. ROAS is faster to measure and good for daily decisions, while ROI tells you whether the whole acquisition system makes money. This calculator bridges them with profit after ad spend.

Why does my ad platform ROAS differ from my store?

Platforms use their own attribution windows and lose events to browser privacy settings and ad blockers. Your store counts every order but cannot tell which ad caused it. Server-side tracking narrows the gap. Use store revenue for profit decisions and platform ROAS to compare campaigns against each other.

Should I include returning customer revenue?

Only if the ads drove that return purchase. For a clean read, calculate ROAS on new-customer revenue and track repeat revenue separately as lifetime value. Mixing them makes prospecting look better than it is and can hide a front end that is quietly losing money on every new buyer.

Can ROAS be too high?

Yes, in practice. A very high ROAS on small spend often means you are under-spending and leaving profitable buyers to competitors. If you sit far above break-even, raise budget in steps of 20 to 30% and watch whether profit after ad spend keeps climbing, even as ROAS drifts down.
