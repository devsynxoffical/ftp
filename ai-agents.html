AI agents for business: which one you need and how they work together

AI agents for business are software assistants that hold real conversations with your leads and customers by phone, text and chat, then take an action: book a call, log a note, route a request or move a deal forward. We build five kinds, all wired into one CRM. This page explains what each does, which one to start with, what it costs to run and the rules we follow so the agents stay honest and compliant.

Quick answer

Which AI agent should a business start with?

Start where leads are leaking today. If new form leads wait for a callback, start with an AI voice agent or appointment setter. If calls go unanswered, start with an AI receptionist. If prospects message you on chat or social, start with a chat agent. If you have a large list of old leads, run a reactivation campaign.

01

The five AI agents we build

  • AI voice agent: calls new leads within about a minute, qualifies them with your questions and books or transfers the good ones.
  • AI appointment setter: runs the whole booking loop by call and SMS, including confirmations, reminders and rebooking no-shows.
  • AI chat agent: replies to website chat, SMS, Facebook Messenger, Instagram DMs and WhatsApp, answers questions from an approved knowledge base and books calls.
  • AI receptionist: answers inbound calls 24/7, texts back missed callers, books after-hours appointments and transfers urgent calls to staff.
  • Lead reactivation: AI SMS and voice campaigns that work through old leads and past customers already sitting in your CRM.

They are not separate products bolted together. Each one reads and writes the same contact record, so a lead who chats on Instagram, misses a call and later answers a reactivation text is one person with one history.

03

How the agents connect to your CRM

We build on GoHighLevel in most cases, because calling, SMS, chat channels, calendars and the CRM pipeline live in one account. Each agent follows the same pattern:

  1. Trigger: a form, an inbound call or message, a pipeline stage or a list upload starts the conversation.
  2. Context: the agent reads the contact record, the source and an approved knowledge base.
  3. Action: it books into a live calendar, tags the contact, moves the pipeline stage or hands off to a person.
  4. Write-back: answers, a short summary and the outcome are saved to the contact so your team sees everything.

The workflows around the agents are covered on our CRM automation page.

04

What AI agents cost to run

The builds are included in our $1,999 complete suite, which covers the CRM, automation, AI booking agent and tracking. Usage is billed by the platforms directly to you. Voice minutes typically run $0.10 to $0.25 per minute of talk time. In our deployments that works out to roughly $3 to $9 per booked appointment, against $40 to $120 per booking for human setters once pay is divided by bookings. Text and chat usage is billed per message or per conversation by the platform and is usually small next to voice. Model your own volume in the AI voice agent ROI calculator and see the trade-offs in AI voice agent vs human setter.

05

Compliance rules every agent follows

  • AI disclosure: voice agents say they are an AI assistant in the opening lines; chat agents do not pretend to be a named human.
  • Consent: outbound calls and texts only go to contacts who gave permission, with the consent source stored on the record.
  • Opt-out: every text explains how to stop messages, and "stop", "unsubscribe" or "do not call" is honored immediately across channels.
  • Calling hours and handoff: calls respect local time windows, and anyone who asks for a person is routed to one.

These rules are summaries of good practice, not legal advice. Your counsel should confirm the rules for your industry and region.

Frequently asked questions.

What is the difference between an AI agent and a chatbot?

A traditional chatbot follows a fixed decision tree of buttons and scripted replies. An AI agent understands free-form questions, answers from an approved knowledge base and takes actions in your systems, such as booking a calendar slot, tagging a contact or transferring a call. We still give agents firm rules, so they never improvise prices or promises.

Do I need all five agents?

No. Most businesses need one or two. A company buying form leads usually starts with a voice agent or appointment setter; a busy clinic or contractor often starts with a receptionist. We recommend the agent that fixes your biggest leak first, then add others once the first one is stable and reviewed.

Which CRM do the agents work with?

We build most agents in GoHighLevel because voice, SMS, chat channels, calendars and pipelines sit in one account, which keeps the setup simple and the data in one place. Other CRMs can work through integrations, but expect more moving parts and a longer testing period before launch.

How much does it cost to build AI agents?

The builds are included in our $1,999 complete suite, alongside the CRM setup, automation, AI booking agent and tracking. Ongoing usage, such as voice minutes, phone numbers and messages, is paid by you directly to the platforms. Voice typically costs $0.10 to $0.25 per minute of talk time.

Will customers know they are talking to AI?

Yes. Our voice agents disclose that they are an AI assistant at the start of each call, and chat agents never claim to be a named human. Disclosure is the honest approach, it is required in a growing number of places, and a fast, useful conversation matters more to prospects than who answers.

How long does it take to launch an AI agent?

A typical agent goes live in 5 to 10 business days. That covers the script or prompt, knowledge base, calendar and CRM connection, handoff rules and internal testing with at least 50 test conversations. After launch we review transcripts weekly for the first month and tighten anything that stalls or confuses people.
