<section id="what-it-is">
  <h2>1. What a Low Ticket Funnel Is</h2>
  <p>A <strong>low ticket funnel</strong> sells an inexpensive product, usually between $7 and $47, directly to cold traffic. Order bumps and one-click upsells lift the average order value until the front end pays for the ads that produced the buyer. When it works, you acquire customers at break-even or better on day zero and make your profit on what they buy next.</p>
  <p>The best known version is the <a href="/glossary/self-liquidating-offer">self-liquidating offer</a>, where the funnel is engineered specifically to liquidate ad spend. Our <a href="/scalelowticket">$847K low-ticket case study</a> is a textbook example: a $27 product, a $17 order bump, and 112% of ad spend recovered on day zero.</p>
  <p>This guide covers how to plan one from scratch. For the detailed economics of that specific build, read the <a href="/blog/low-ticket-self-liquidating-offer-funnel-847k-breakdown">$847K self-liquidating offer breakdown</a>. If you plan to hire someone to build it, see our comparison of a <a href="/compare/funnel-agency-vs-freelancer">sales funnel freelancer vs a funnel agency</a>.</p>
</section>

<section id="choose-product">
  <h2>2. Choosing the Front-End Product</h2>
  <p>The front-end product decides almost everything downstream. It should pass four tests:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Immediate use.</strong> The buyer gets value the same day. Templates, swipe files, short trainings and toolkits work. Twelve-week courses do not.</li>
    <li><strong>One clear problem.</strong> The product solves a narrow, painful problem completely, rather than a broad one partially.</li>
    <li><strong>A natural next step.</strong> Using it should create demand for your main offer, not replace it.</li>
    <li><strong>Provable in an ad.</strong> One screenshot, result or before-and-after should make the price feel obvious.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Communities and memberships can also work as front ends. Several of our <a href="/case-studies/low-ticket-funnels">low-ticket funnel case studies</a> sold memberships, including one that sold <a href="/case-studies/low-ticket-funnel-1566-memberships-sold">1,566 memberships</a>. A low monthly price lowers the barrier, and retention becomes the backend.</p>
  <p>A simple way to test product ideas before building anything: write the ad first. If you cannot describe the product, the result it delivers and why it is worth the price in a short ad, cold buyers will not understand it either. Then ask a handful of past customers or followers whether they would pay for it today. If the answers are lukewarm, change the problem the product solves rather than lowering the price. Cheaper rarely fixes an offer nobody wants; a sharper promise usually does. Finally, decide the backend offer at the same time, because the front-end product should lead straight into it.</p>
</section>

<section id="pricing">
  <h2>3. Pricing: Why $7 to $47</h2>
  <p>Price is a filter as much as a revenue number. Too low and the list fills with people who will never buy again. Too high and cold traffic needs more proof than an ad can deliver, so conversion falls and the funnel stops liquidating.</p>
  <div class="table-wrap">
    <table class="data-table">
      <thead>
        <tr><th>Price</th><th>Typical role</th><th>Trade-off</th></tr>
      </thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>$7 to $17</td><td>Impulse buy, high volume</td><td>Easy to sell; buyers can be lower intent</td></tr>
        <tr class="highlight-row"><td><strong>$27</strong></td><td><strong>Our most common front end</strong></td><td><strong>Balances conversion and buyer quality</strong></td></tr>
        <tr><td>$37 to $47</td><td>Higher-value toolkit or workshop</td><td>Needs stronger proof on the sales page</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
  <p>In the $847K build, $27 was deliberate. Lower prices brought in buyers who rarely ascended, and higher prices cut conversion enough to break the day-zero math.</p>
</section>

<section id="page-sequence">
  <h2>4. The Page Sequence</h2>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Page 1</div>
    <h3>Sales page</h3>
    <p>Lead with the result, show proof early, explain exactly what the buyer gets, and anchor the price against the value. A short video helps. Our <a href="/templates/funnel-copy-template">funnel copy template</a> has the section order.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Page 2</div>
    <h3>Checkout with an order bump</h3>
    <p>Use a two-step checkout, contact details then payment, so abandoned carts can be followed up. Add one <a href="/glossary/order-bump">order bump</a>: a complementary item priced around half the front end, sold with a single checkbox.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Page 3</div>
    <h3>One-click upsell</h3>
    <p>A deeper or faster version of what they just bought, usually a few times the front-end price, purchased without re-entering card details.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Page 4</div>
    <h3>Downsell</h3>
    <p>A lighter version for those who declined the upsell. The <a href="/glossary/upsell-and-downsell">upsell and downsell</a> entry explains how to structure the pair.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Page 5</div>
    <h3>Thank-you and onboarding</h3>
    <p>Deliver the product, tell buyers what to expect next, and introduce the backend without hard-selling it yet.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="aov-math">
  <h2>5. The Math: AOV vs Cost Per Buyer</h2>
  <p>One rule governs every low ticket funnel: <strong>average order value must be at least equal to cost per buyer.</strong> If it is, the funnel self-liquidates. If not, you are paying to acquire customers and need a backend that pays it back quickly.</p>
  <div class="callout-box">
    <div class="callout-badge">How to calculate it</div>
    <p>AOV = front-end price + (bump price x bump take rate) + (upsell price x upsell take rate) + (downsell price x downsell take rate among decliners). Compare it with ad spend divided by number of buyers.</p>
  </div>
  <p>In the $847K funnel, average order value reached $68.40 after optimization, against a cost per buyer that made the front end recover 112% of spend on day zero. Every improvement after break-even, a sharper bump headline or a video on the upsell page, went straight to margin.</p>
  <p>Use the <a href="/tools/roas-calculator">ROAS calculator</a> to see what return you need at your price points, and the <a href="/tools/funnel-conversion-calculator">funnel conversion calculator</a> to test how bump and upsell take rates change the outcome.</p>
</section>

  <div class="table-wrap">

</section>

<section id="tracking-and-scaling">
  <h2>7. Tracking and Scaling</h2>
  <p>A low ticket funnel lives or dies on how well the ad platform understands who is buying. Send every purchase, including bumps and upsells, as a server-side purchase event with its value. That lets Meta optimize for buyers with higher order values rather than the cheapest sale. Our <a href="/meta-conversions-api-setup">Conversions API setups</a> recovered 31% more events than the pixel alone.</p>
  <p>Scale in steps once the funnel is liquidating. Results from our clients show what is possible when the math holds:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="/case-studies/low-ticket-funnel-132-sales">132 sales on $1,214.35 of ad spend</a> at an 11.7x return, a small-budget test that proved the offer.</li>
    <li><a href="/case-studies/low-ticket-funnel-58108-in-one-day-at-4-52x">$58,108 in one day at 4.52x</a> on $12,862 of spend.</li>
    <li><a href="/case-studies/low-ticket-funnel-135k-revenue-on-36k-ad-spend">$135,143 in revenue from 892 sales</a> on $36,198 of spend.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Watch front-end conversion rate, bump take rate and cost per buyer daily as you scale. When cost per buyer climbs past AOV, refresh creative before raising budget again.</p>
</section>

<section id="numbers-to-watch">
  <h2>8. The Numbers to Watch Every Day</h2>
  <div class="table-wrap">
    <table class="data-table">

  <p>Check these daily during the first two weeks and weekly after that. Most problems show up in one number long before they show up in revenue.</p>

<section id="wrong-choice">
  <h2>9. When a Low Ticket Funnel Is the Wrong Choice</h2>
  <p>This model is powerful but not universal. It tends to struggle when:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>There is no backend.</strong> Without a next offer, the best case is break-even and the worst case is a slow loss.</li>
    <li><strong>The product needs a conversation.</strong> If buyers need questions answered before they trust you, a booked-call funnel will usually work better.</li>
    <li><strong>You sell local services.</strong> Roofers, HVAC companies and clinics generally do better with a short lead form and instant follow-up than with a paid digital product.</li>
    <li><strong>Cash flow cannot wait.</strong> If the funnel liquidates at less than 100%, the backend has to close the gap, and that can take weeks.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>If one of these describes you, look at our <a href="/sales-funnel-agency">sales funnel agency</a> options for booked-call and lead funnels instead.</p>
</section>
