<section id="what-you-pay-for">
  <h2>1. What You Are Actually Paying For</h2>
  <p><strong>How much does a sales funnel cost?</strong> Building it yourself costs $0 beyond a funnel builder subscription, a freelancer typically charges a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, and a full-service agency charges several thousand to five figures, often with a monthly retainer. Our fixed builds are $999 to $1,999 one-time. Where you land depends less on the pages and more on everything around them. A sales funnel is the full path from ad click to sale: the landing page or quiz, the offer, the checkout or booking calendar, the follow-up that reaches the lead, and the tracking that tells your ad platform which leads became customers. A quote that only covers pages is pricing a fraction of the system.</p>
  <p>Since 2019 we have built and documented more than 275 client results, from $5 roofing leads to a <a href="/milliondollarcasestudy">$1.52M high-ticket consulting funnel</a>. The funnels that paid back fastest were rarely the most expensive. They were the ones where the build included the parts that most quotes leave out: instant follow-up, reminders and server-side tracking.</p>
  <p>If you are new to the terminology, our <a href="/glossary/sales-funnel">sales funnel glossary entry</a> explains each stage. This guide focuses on money: what each option costs, what drives the number up, and how to judge whether a funnel is worth it before you sign anything.</p>
</section>

<section id="cost-drivers">
  <h2>3. The Five Factors That Drive Funnel Cost</h2>
  <p>When two quotes differ by ten times, the difference almost always comes from these five line items.</p>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Factor 1</div>
    <h3>Copywriting</h3>
    <p>Copy is the single biggest variable in conversion and in price. A funnel built on copy you supply is cheaper to quote but often converts worse. Our <a href="/templates/funnel-copy-template">funnel copy template</a> shows the structure we write to if you want to draft it yourself.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Factor 2</div>
    <h3>Number and type of pages</h3>
    <p>A single opt-in page is quick. A <a href="/vsl-funnels">VSL funnel</a> with an application, a <a href="/quiz-funnels">quiz funnel</a> with branching logic, or a checkout with an order bump and two upsells takes longer to plan, build and test.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Factor 3</div>
    <h3>CRM and automation depth</h3>
    <p>Pipeline stages, instant SMS and email, missed-call text-back, round-robin routing and reminder sequences are where most of the build hours go, and where most of the revenue is recovered. See what this covers on our <a href="/crm-automation">CRM automation</a> page.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Factor 4</div>
    <h3>Tracking</h3>
    <p>Pixel-only tracking is cheap to set up and increasingly unreliable. Server-side events through the <a href="/meta-conversions-api-setup">Meta Conversions API</a> take more work but recovered 31% more events in our builds, which changes what the ad algorithm learns from.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card">
    <div class="step-badge">Factor 5</div>
    <h3>Ownership and retainers</h3>
    <p>Some agencies build inside their own accounts and charge monthly for access. That lowers the upfront number but raises lifetime cost and makes leaving painful. Ask who owns the funnel, CRM and ad account before you compare prices.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="hidden-costs">
  <h2>5. The Monthly Costs Nobody Quotes</h2>
  <p>The build fee is a one-off. These are the recurring costs that should sit in your budget from day one:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Funnel and CRM software.</strong> One all-in-one platform or several separate tools. Our <a href="/blog/best-funnel-builder-2026">best funnel builder comparison</a> covers current plans.</li>
    <li><strong>Usage fees.</strong> SMS, email sends and phone minutes are usually billed on top of the subscription.</li>
    <li><strong>AI calling.</strong> If you use an <a href="/ai-voice-agents">AI voice agent</a>, expect roughly $0.10 to $0.25 per minute, which worked out to about $3 to $9 per booked appointment in our deployments versus $40 to $120 with human setters.</li>
    <li><strong>Ad spend.</strong> The funnel needs enough traffic to learn. Underfunding the test is the most common reason a good funnel gets labelled a bad one.</li>
    <li><strong>Optimization time.</strong> Someone has to read the numbers weekly and change headlines, offers and follow-up timing.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The cost that never appears on an invoice is the lead you paid for and never reached. Our <a href="/speed-to-lead">speed-to-lead</a> page explains why that one number usually outweighs the build fee.</p>

<section id="payback-math">
  <h2>6. Payback Math: Will the Funnel Pay for Itself?</h2>
  <p>Before any build, we run a simple payback model. You need four inputs: monthly ad spend, cost per lead, lead-to-sale rate and average sale value. Plug them into our <a href="/tools/funnel-conversion-calculator">funnel conversion calculator</a> and change one input at a time to see which lever matters most for your offer.</p>
  <div class="callout-box">
    <div class="callout-badge">Worked example</div>
    <p>A service business spends $5,000 a month at $25 per lead, so 200 leads. If 10% book and 50% of those close at $2,000, that is 10 sales and $20,000. Lift lead-to-booking by even half of the 2.4x we have measured and the same ad spend produces 15 sales. A $1,999 build pays back inside the first month.</p>
  </div>
  <p>Real numbers from our case studies show the range. A roofing client generated <a href="/case-studies/roofing-537-leads-at-4-27-cpl">537 leads at $4.27 each</a> on $2,292 of spend. A med spa turned <a href="/case-studies/med-spa-12k-revenue-on-590-ad-spend">$590 of ad spend into $11,700</a> in revenue. On the low-ticket side, our <a href="/scalelowticket">$847K front-end funnel</a> recovered 112% of ad spend on day zero, so the funnel paid for its own traffic before any backend sale.</p>
  <p>If your current cost per lead is the problem, the <a href="/tools/cost-per-lead-calculator">cost per lead calculator</a> shows how much a lower CPL is worth at your close rate.</p>
</section>

<section id="build-timeline">
  <h2>7. What Happens During a 5 to 10 Day Build</h2>
  <p>Part of what you pay for is speed and certainty. A slow build costs money too, because every week without a working funnel is a week of ad spend going to a weaker page or no campaign at all. Here is how a typical build runs on our side:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Day 1: strategy call and scope.</strong> We confirm the offer, the audience, the funnel type and where the funnel ends: a call, an appointment or a checkout.</li>
    <li><strong>Days 2 to 4: copy and page build.</strong> Headlines, proof, forms or quiz logic, checkout or calendar. You review one draft instead of five rounds of feedback.</li>
    <li><strong>Days 4 to 7: CRM and automation.</strong> Pipeline stages, instant SMS and email, call attempts, reminders and handoff rules to your team.</li>
    <li><strong>Days 6 to 9: tracking and testing.</strong> Pixel and server-side events, test leads through every branch, and a check that each stage fires the right event.</li>
    <li><strong>Day 10 at the latest: launch.</strong> You get logins, a walkthrough and full ownership of every account.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>When a quote says six to eight weeks for a similar scope, most of that time is usually waiting on copy, approvals or integrations. Ask what the timeline depends on before you accept it, and get it in writing.</p>
</section>

<section id="red-flags">
  <h2>8. Red Flags in Funnel Quotes</h2>
  <p>Whether you hire us or anyone else, these are the signs that a low quote will turn into a high cost later:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>No mention of follow-up.</strong> If the quote covers pages but says nothing about what happens after someone opts in, leads will sit in an inbox.</li>
    <li><strong>The agency keeps the accounts.</strong> If you cannot export your contacts, funnels and workflows when you leave, you are renting, not buying.</li>
    <li><strong>Guaranteed results with no numbers behind them.</strong> Ask for case studies with ad spend, leads and revenue, not just testimonials.</li>
    <li><strong>Tracking described as "pixel installed".</strong> Browser-only tracking misses a large share of conversions. Ask about server-side events.</li>
    <li><strong>Unlimited revisions with no defined scope.</strong> It sounds generous but usually means the project has no end date.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section id="how-to-choose">
  <h2>9. How to Choose the Right Budget Level</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>You are pre-revenue and testing an idea:</strong> start DIY on an inexpensive builder and validate the offer with a small ad budget.</li>
    <li><strong>You have proven copy and a CRM you like:</strong> a design-only build is enough.</li>
    <li><strong>You sell something that needs a conversation:</strong> pay for copy, follow-up and tracking. Without them, the pages will generate leads that never turn into calls.</li>
    <li><strong>You sell a low-priced product online:</strong> prioritize checkout, order bump and upsell structure. Our <a href="/blog/low-ticket-funnel-guide">low ticket funnel guide</a> covers the economics.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Whichever level you pick, ask every provider the same three questions: what exactly is in scope, who owns the accounts after launch, and what happens in the first 60 seconds after someone opts in. To see examples by industry, browse our <a href="/case-studies">case studies</a> or the <a href="/blog/sales-funnel-examples">sales funnel examples</a> roundup. When you are ready, <a href="/book-a-call">book a strategy call</a> and we will run the payback math on your numbers.</p>
