Facebook Ads Cost Per Lead: Benchmarks From 48 Real Campaigns, by Industry

What Meta lead generation campaigns actually paid per lead, campaign by campaign: a $11.13 median, industry ranges, what makes a cost per lead good, and why it climbs.

1. The Short Answer

Across 48 Meta (Facebook and Instagram) lead generation campaigns we ran and published, the median Facebook ads cost per lead was $11.13. The cheapest campaign paid $4.27 per lead and the most expensive paid $35.00. In the 30 campaigns that published both their spend and their lead count, $898,489 of ad spend bought 105,828 leads, a blended cost per lead of $8.49.

44% of the campaigns came in under $10 per lead and 77% under $20. These are leads from our own funnels: landing pages or quizzes with qualifying questions, most with phone verification, followed by instant calls and texts. They are not survey estimates, and every figure below links to the case study it comes from.

The number that decides whether your own cost per lead is good is not on any benchmark table. It is your break-even cost per lead, which section 4 explains.

4. What Is a Good Cost Per Lead on Facebook Ads?

A good cost per lead is one below the most you can afford to pay. That ceiling is your break-even cost per lead:

Break-even cost per lead = revenue per customer × gross margin × lead-to-customer rate

BusinessRevenue per customerGross marginLead-to-customer rateBreak-even CPL
Roofing replacement$12,00030%3%$108
Med spa first treatment$60060%15%$54
Mortgage refinance$3,00070%2%$42
Coaching program$3,00080%4%$96

These are illustrative inputs, not client figures; put your own into the cost per lead calculator. The point is that a $11.13 lead can be wildly profitable for one business and a loss for another. If your cost per lead sits well below break-even, scale spend even if cost per lead rises. If it sits above, the fix is usually a higher lead-to-customer rate rather than a cheaper lead: doubling the close rate doubles what you can pay.

5. Why Cost Per Lead Varies So Much

  • Offer. "Free roof inspection after the storm" and "Get a quote" target the same homeowner at very different prices. The offer is the biggest lever we see.
  • Qualification. Every extra question and phone verification step removes some leads. Cost per lead rises, cost per booked call usually falls. Our cheapest leads are not always our most profitable.
  • Form type. Instant forms on Facebook are cheaper per lead than a landing page funnel and lower in intent. We compare them in Facebook lead ads vs funnels.
  • Market and season. Storm season for roofing, rate changes for mortgage and open enrollment for insurance all change competition in the auction.
  • Creative. Real footage of the crew, the clinic or the founder usually beats stock imagery, and fresh angles hold cost per lead down longer.
  • Signal. Campaigns that send booked calls and sales back to Meta through the Conversions API give the algorithm better data to find buyers.

6. Cost Per Lead vs Cost Per Acquisition

MetricFormulaUse it to
Cost per lead (CPL)Ad spend ÷ leadsCompare ads and audiences quickly
Cost per qualified leadAd spend ÷ leads that pass qualificationStrip out junk and duplicate leads
Cost per booked callAd spend ÷ booked callsJudge follow-up and intent
Cost per acquisition (CPA or CAC)Ad spend ÷ new customers, or CPL ÷ lead-to-customer rateDecide budget and profitability

Cost per lead arrives first, so it is the number everyone watches. Cost per acquisition is the number that pays the bills. A $20 lead that closes at 5% is a $400 customer; a $35 lead that closes at 12% is a $292 customer. That is why we send qualified events back to Meta and judge every test on cost per booked call or sale. The cost per lead glossary entry covers which leads to count.

7. Why Does Cost Per Lead Increase?

  1. Creative fatigue. Frequency climbs, click-through falls and Meta charges more to reach the same people. Refresh angles before performance drops, not after.
  2. Audience saturation. Small local markets run out of new people quickly. Broaden the radius or the offer rather than narrowing targeting further.
  3. Lost signal. Browser privacy limits and ad blockers hide conversions from the pixel. Server-side tracking recovered 31% more events on our builds.
  4. Learning resets. Large budget jumps and frequent edits push ad sets back into learning. Scale in steps of 20 to 30%.
  5. Seasonal competition. Q4 retail spend and industry seasons raise auction prices for everyone.
  6. A slower page. A new script or heavy chat widget can cut conversion rate, which shows up as higher cost per lead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost per lead on Facebook ads?

Across the 48 Meta lead generation campaigns we have published, the median cost per lead was $11.13 and the blended figure, total spend divided by total leads, was $8.49. Industry medians ranged from about $8 to $15. Treat any average as a reference point, then compare it with your own break-even cost per lead.

What is a good cost per lead for Facebook ads?

Any cost per lead comfortably below your break-even cost per lead, which is revenue per customer times gross margin times your lead-to-customer rate. A $40 lead is cheap for a business that closes 10% of leads at $5,000, and a $5 lead is expensive if nobody buys. The cost per lead calculator works it out for you.

Why is my Facebook ads cost per lead so high?

The usual causes are a weak offer, creative fatigue, a narrow or saturated audience, a slow landing page and broken tracking that starves Meta of conversion data. Seasonality and competition matter too. Check the offer and first screen first, then make sure leads and booked calls are reaching Meta through the Conversions API.

Are Facebook lead ads cheaper than a landing page funnel?

Instant forms usually produce a lower cost per lead because they are prefilled and never leave Facebook. They also produce more low-intent and mistyped leads. A funnel with qualifying questions and phone verification costs more per lead but often less per booked call. Compare the two on cost per sale, not cost per lead.

How many leads do I need before trusting my cost per lead?

Cost per lead settles quickly, often after a few hundred dollars of spend and 30 to 50 leads per ad set. Lead quality takes longer, because you need leads to book, attend and buy. Give a new campaign at least one full sales cycle before judging it on cost per customer.
