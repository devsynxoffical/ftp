Quiz Funnel Examples: 7 Real Builds and Patterns With Questions, Result Pages and Follow-Up

Four quiz and estimator funnels from real client builds with their numbers, three reusable patterns clearly labeled as patterns, and the question structures, result pages and follow-up that make each one work.

1. What Makes a Good Quiz Funnel

The quiz funnel examples in this article come from two places. Four are real client builds from our case studies, with the ad spend and results attached. Three are reusable patterns we use when scoping new builds, and they are labeled as patterns because they are not tied to a single client result.

Every quiz funnel that worked for us shared the same skeleton:

  • A specific promise in the ad and headline, such as an estimate, a case review or a personalized plan, rather than "take our quiz".
  • Three to six easy multiple-choice questions, one per screen, with a progress bar.
  • Contact details last, after the visitor has invested in the answers, with phone verification where junk leads are a risk.
  • A result page with one next step: book, buy or talk.
  • Follow-up that uses the answers, starting within the first minute.

Our lead qualification questions template lists the questions that do the most sorting work by industry, and the quiz funnel service page shows how we build them.

2. Roofing Estimator: 537 Leads at $4.27

Type: real client build, local home services.

Question structure: a short estimator behind a Meta ad. What does the roof need (repair, replacement, storm damage, not sure)? What type of property? How soon do you want the work done? Is the homeowner the decision-maker? Then name, address and phone, verified by a text code.

Result page: confirms the estimate request and tells the homeowner exactly what happens next and when.

Follow-up: an instant text and a call attempt within a minute, using the job type from the answers.

Result: 537 leads at $4.27 cost per lead on $2,292 of ad spend. Phone verification kept the cheap leads real. More builds are on the roofing case studies hub.

3. MVA Qualification Quiz: $388K on $20K

Type: real client build, personal injury.

Question structure: a multi-step free case review. When did the accident happen? Were you injured and did you get medical treatment? Were you at fault? Do you already have a lawyer? Each answer screens out leads the firm cannot take, before intake staff spend time on them.

Result page: tells qualified claimants an intake specialist is calling now, and gives everyone else a polite exit.

Follow-up: an instant intake call to verified, qualified leads.

Result: 1,079 leads at $18.94, producing $388,000 in revenue on $20,324 of ad spend, a 19.09x return. A separate MVA build with the same multi-step approach delivered 1,865 leads at $5.24.

Lesson: qualification questions may raise cost per lead slightly, but they lower cost per signed case, which is the number that matters.

4. Med Spa Treatment Quiz: 1,011 Leads

Type: real client build, aesthetics.

Question structure: a few quick taps on treatment interest, the area of concern, whether the visitor has had the treatment before and how soon they want to book. Short, visual and mobile-first, because med spa traffic is almost entirely on phones.

Result page: recommends the matching treatment and offer with a booking link.

Follow-up: a fast text with the offer and calendar, then appointment reminders.

Result: 1,011 leads on $4,703 of ad spend. In another med spa build, $590 of spend produced $11,700 in revenue from 45 leads. See med spa funnels for the full structure.

5. Coaching Diagnostic: 174 Appointments

Type: real client build, high-ticket coaching.

Question structure: a diagnostic-style application. Where is the business today? What is the main bottleneck? What have you tried? How soon do you want to fix it, and are you able to invest if it is a fit? The last two questions do the qualifying.

Result page: a short summary of the bottleneck and a calendar for qualified applicants, with a video that explains the approach before the call.

Follow-up: instant confirmation, a call to confirm the booking, and three-channel reminders.

Result: 174 appointments and $115,500 in revenue on $2,214 of ad spend. More are on the coaching and consulting case studies hub.

6. Three Reusable Patterns (Not Client Builds)

The following are patterns, not client results. Use them as starting structures and measure your own numbers.

Pattern 1

Product finder

For: ecommerce stores with more than a handful of products. Questions: who it is for, the main goal or problem, preferences such as skin type or size, budget. Result page: one primary recommendation with a direct add-to-cart button, plus one alternative. Follow-up: an email that repeats the recommendation and a reminder for abandoned carts.

Pattern 2

Assessment or scorecard

For: B2B services, agencies and consultants. Questions: eight to twelve quick yes-or-no or scale questions about current practices. Result page: a score, the two weakest areas and an offer to review the results on a call. Follow-up: a short email series based on the lowest-scoring area.

Pattern 3

Calculator

For: offers with a clear financial outcome, such as solar savings, mortgage payments or cost of no-shows. Questions: a few numeric inputs. Result page: the calculated figure and what it would take to improve it. Our free no-show cost calculator is an example of the format.

8. How to Write Quiz Questions

Across the four client builds above, the questions followed the same rules, whatever the industry.

  1. Start with the easiest, most interesting question. "What does your roof need?" or "Which area would you like to treat?" is about the visitor, not about you, and it takes one tap to answer.
  2. Every question must change something. If an answer does not affect the result page, the routing or the first follow-up message, delete the question. Each extra screen costs completions.
  3. Put qualifying questions in the middle. Timing, decision-maker and budget questions work best after two or three easy answers, once the visitor is committed.
  4. Offer four or five answers at most, including an honest "not sure" option so nobody stalls.
  5. Ask for contact details last, framed as where to send the result, estimate or case review.
  6. Verify the phone number when lead quality matters. A text code filters typos and fake numbers before your team dials them.

Write the questions in the customer's words, not your internal labels. A homeowner says "my roof is leaking", not "repair inquiry". Our funnel copy template has prompts for the headline and result page copy that sit around the questions.

9. Result Pages and Follow-Up That Convert

A quiz earns trust by asking good questions. The result page and follow-up are where that trust either becomes a sale or leaks away.

  • Reflect the answers. Name the job type, injury, treatment or bottleneck the visitor chose. A generic thank-you page throws away the personalization you just collected.
  • One call to action. A calendar, a checkout button or a "we are calling you now" message. Not three options.
  • Add proof near the button. One relevant result or testimonial is enough.
  • Use the answers in the first message. Pass every answer into the CRM as a field so the first text and call can reference it. Our speed-to-lead layer reached 84% of leads inside the first minute across 60+ deployments and lifted lead-to-booking 2.4x.
  • Send answers to your ad platform. Qualified answers make better conversion events than raw opt-ins, which helps ads find more people like your best leads.

10. Common Quiz Funnel Mistakes

When a quiz funnel underperforms, it is usually one of these:

  • A trivia-style promise. "Which type of entrepreneur are you?" attracts curiosity clicks, not buyers. Promise something the buyer actually wants: an estimate, a case review, a plan.
  • Too many questions. Ten screens for a local service is too many. Cut until every question earns its place.
  • Asking for contact details first. It turns the quiz back into a form and loses the commitment effect.
  • A result page with no next step. If the result does not lead straight to a booking or checkout, most visitors leave.
  • Answers that never reach the CRM. The sales team calls blind and asks the same questions again, which annoys the lead and wastes the quiz.
  • Slow follow-up. A quiz lead is a warm lead for minutes, not days. The speed to lead principle applies here more than anywhere.

Fix these and a quiz is one of the most reliable front ends for cold traffic we build.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a quiz funnel?

A quiz funnel replaces a plain opt-in form with a short series of questions. The visitor answers three to six multiple-choice questions, enters contact details at the end and sees a personalized result with one next step, such as booking a call or claiming an offer. The answers also qualify and segment the lead.

How many questions should a quiz funnel have?

Three to six for most lead generation offers. Local services do well with three or four quick questions, while high-ticket diagnostics can stretch to six or seven if each question feels relevant. Put the easiest questions first, ask for contact details last, and remove any question you will not use in follow-up or routing.

Do quiz funnels work for local businesses?

Yes, usually as estimators. A roofing or HVAC estimator asks about the job, the property and timing, then promises a quote. One of our roofing builds generated 537 leads at $4.27 each. The key is fast follow-up, because a homeowner who just requested an estimate is often comparing several contractors at once.

What should a quiz result page include?

A result that reflects the answers, a short explanation of why it fits, one piece of proof and a single call to action. For booked-call offers, put the calendar on the result page. For products, show the recommended item with a direct checkout button. Generic results that ignore the answers waste the trust the quiz built.

Is a quiz funnel better than a VSL funnel?

Neither wins everywhere. Quizzes tend to convert cold traffic into leads more efficiently and qualify as they go, while a VSL does more selling for complex, high-priced offers. Many of our high-ticket builds combine both, with a short diagnostic first and a video on the result page before the calendar.

Which tools can build a quiz funnel?

Most funnel builders and CRMs, including GoHighLevel, support multi-step forms with conditional logic, and dedicated quiz tools exist too. The tool matters less than the connection: answers must flow into the CRM as fields and tags so follow-up messages, routing and ad conversion events can use them immediately.
