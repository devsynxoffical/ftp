01

Funnel structure

These terms describe the shape of a funnel: how a stranger becomes a lead and how the lead is moved toward a sale.

  • Sales funnel: the sequence of steps that turns ad traffic into leads, calls and customers.
  • Lead magnet: the free resource, quiz result or estimate you trade for contact details.
  • VSL (video sales letter): a scripted video that sells the offer or the call before the prospect books.

If you want a funnel built rather than defined, start with our sales funnel agency page or the guide to sales funnel stages.

02

Offers and order value

Offer terms explain how a funnel pays for its own ads and raises the value of every buyer.

  • Tripwire offer: a low-priced first purchase that turns a lead into a customer.
  • Self-liquidating offer: a front end built so revenue covers the ad spend that bought it.
  • Order bump: a checkbox add-on on the checkout page.
  • Upsell and downsell: the one-click offers shown after the first purchase.

All four appear in our $847K low-ticket funnel case study, which pairs a $27 product with a $17 bump.

03

Traffic and tracking

Tracking terms decide whether the ad platform learns who buys or only who clicks.

  • Meta Pixel: the browser code that reports page views, leads and purchases to Meta.
  • Conversions API: the server-to-server connection that sends the same events, plus offline ones, without relying on the browser.
  • Cost per lead: ad spend divided by leads, the first number most funnels are judged on.

Our Conversions API setup service connects both, and the cost per lead calculator shows what your numbers mean.

04

Follow-up, CRM and AI

Most funnels lose value after the form. These terms describe the systems that recover it.

  • Speed to lead: the time between a form submission and the first contact.
  • Show rate: the share of booked calls that actually happen.
  • CRM pipeline: the stages a lead moves through from new to won.
  • AI voice agent: software that calls, qualifies and books leads by phone.
  • GoHighLevel snapshot: a saved copy of a full account setup that loads into a new sub-account.

See how they connect in speed-to-lead automation, AI voice agents and CRM automation.

Frequently asked questions.

Who is this sales funnel glossary for?

It is written for business owners, marketers and agency operators who buy or build funnels and want to understand what their agency, software or dashboard is talking about. Each definition avoids jargon where possible and uses real numbers from our client results so the terms connect to outcomes, not theory.

Where do the example numbers come from?

Every result quoted in the glossary comes from a documented Funnels That Print case study, such as the $1.52M consulting funnel, the $847K low-ticket funnel or our speed-to-lead data across 60+ deployments. Where a page shows a calculation with round numbers, it is labeled as an illustration rather than a client result.

Which glossary terms matter most for lead generation?

For lead generation, start with cost per lead, speed to lead and show rate. Cost per lead tells you what the ads cost, speed to lead decides how many leads you actually reach, and show rate decides how many booked calls turn into sales conversations. The Conversions API then feeds those outcomes back to Meta.

Which terms matter most for selling products online?

For product and course funnels, focus on tripwire offer, order bump, upsell and downsell, and self-liquidating offer. Together they describe how a low-priced front end raises average order value enough to pay for its own ads, which is how our $847K funnel recovered 112% of ad spend on day zero.

Do I need to know all these terms to work with you?

No. We explain everything on a strategy call and in the build plan. The glossary is here so you can check our reasoning, compare agencies on equal terms and read your own dashboard with confidence. Knowing what a good show rate or cost per lead looks like helps you spot problems early.
