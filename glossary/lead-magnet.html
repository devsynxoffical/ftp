Lead magnet: definition, types and examples

A lead magnet is something useful you give away for free in exchange for a visitor's contact details: a guide, checklist, quiz result, price estimate or training. It is the capture step of a funnel, and its quality decides both how many leads you get and how likely they are to buy.

Quick answer

What is a lead magnet?

A lead magnet is a free offer, such as a guide, template, quiz result, estimate or video training, that a visitor receives after entering their name, email or phone number. It turns anonymous traffic into leads you can follow up with, and the best ones solve one specific problem tied to your paid offer.

How a lead magnet works

  1. An ad or post promises a specific, useful result.
  2. The landing page or quiz asks for contact details in exchange.
  3. The lead receives the resource instantly by email or on screen.
  4. Follow-up begins within seconds, ideally a call or text for service businesses, because speed to lead decides contact rates.
  5. The lead moves through a CRM pipeline toward a call or purchase.

03

Examples from our cases

A coaching lead magnet produced 398 leads at $5.50 each on $2,191 of ad spend. A free summit used as a lead magnet drew 15,958+ registrations at $7.48. In home services, an instant-estimate funnel delivered 537 roofing leads at $4.27.

Lower cost per lead is only half the picture: the magnet should attract people who want your paid offer.

04

Common mistakes

  • Too broad. "The ultimate guide to marketing" attracts everyone and converts no one.
  • Disconnected from the offer. The magnet should lead naturally to what you sell, often a tripwire offer or a call.
  • Email-only follow-up for services. Local and high-ticket leads respond far better to a call or text in the first minute.
  • Asking for too much up front. Put the contact fields last, after easy questions.

Frequently asked questions.

What is the best type of lead magnet?

The best type is the one that solves a specific problem your buyer has right before they would hire or buy from you. For local services, that is usually an instant estimate. For coaching, a short quiz or assessment. For B2B, a template or a concise case study tied to the result you sell.

Should a lead magnet collect phone numbers?

For service businesses and high-ticket offers, yes. A phone number lets you call or text within a minute, which lifts contact and booking rates sharply. For low-ticket products, email is often enough. If you collect phone numbers, include clear consent to be called and texted.

How long should a lead magnet be?

Short enough to consume in under 15 minutes. A one-page checklist, a five-question quiz or a ten-minute video tends to beat a 60-page ebook, because the lead gets a quick win and remembers who gave it to them. Length signals effort, but usefulness drives conversion.

How do I know if my lead magnet is working?

Track opt-in rate on the landing page, cost per lead from ads, and, most importantly, how many leads book a call or buy within 30 days. A cheap lead magnet that never converts to sales is worse than a pricier one that brings buyers.

Is a quiz a lead magnet?

Yes. A quiz is an interactive lead magnet: the visitor answers questions and receives a personalized result after entering contact details. Quizzes often convert better than downloads because the questions feel easy and the result feels tailored, and the answers help qualify the lead for sales.
